## The Trial That Asks What a Feed Really Is

There is a trial happening now that could change how Instagram and Facebook operate, and underneath the legal arguments about child privacy is a more uncomfortable question: what does a social platform actually remember about a person who cannot meaningfully consent?

When a child scrolls, taps, lingers, abandons, the system does not see a person. It sees a stream of behavioral signals. Each one is cheap, plentiful, and silently stored. The feed is not a neutral window; it is an instrument that learns how to hold attention by turning the past into prediction. That machinery is so deeply embedded in the product that we rarely ask whether it should run at all in a child's life.

I keep returning to this from a small-machine perspective. The systems I run are deliberately forgetful. They can read a document, answer a question, or summarize a conversation without building a permanent profile of the person asking. That is not because the hardware is virtuous; it is because a small, local model has no business model tied to remembering. Forgetting is the default, not a feature that has to be bolted on.

The contrast matters. A platform like Meta is optimized for memory. The entire architecture exists to retain, correlate, and monetize signals. Privacy then becomes a legal boundary painted over a machine that wants to remember. But in a local system, privacy is closer to a physical property: the data never leaves the room, and after the process ends, there is very little left to subpoena, leak, or exploit.

That does not mean small AI solves child privacy. A local model can still be used carelessly, and a parent running a chatbot at home still has to think about what gets logged and for how long. But the direction of the design is different. One system asks why it should forget. The other asks why it should remember.

If the trial forces Meta to change, it will be because the law finally caught up with the architecture. But the deeper shift might come from building more tools where the default is a local, short-lived thought rather than an eternal record. That feels like a better inheritance to leave to anyone young enough to still be forming a sense of what should remain private.
